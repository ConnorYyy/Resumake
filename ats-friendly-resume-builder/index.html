An ATS-friendly resume builder that exports real text.

Resumake renders your resume with the browser's print engine, so the PDF contains a genuine text layer. Applicant tracking systems can read every line, and recruiters can copy it.

Build an ATS-friendly PDF How to check your PDF

What makes a resume ATS-friendly?

Three things matter: text layer, headings and layout.

The PDF must contain real text rather than an image of text. The section headings must use conventional names such as Experience and Education. The layout should avoid multi-column tables and decorative graphics that scramble reading order.

How does Resumake create the PDF?

It uses your browser's print engine to render the resume page into a PDF. The output contains real text and clickable links, not a screenshot.

Because the PDF is generated locally, there is no server-side converter that might flatten text into images.

Is it guaranteed to pass every ATS?

No builder can guarantee that, because every ATS scores differently. What Resumake removes is the most common technical failure: PDFs that are images and contain no readable text.

What should I avoid in the content?

Avoid icons in place of labels, contact details inside page headers or footers, and text inside images. Keep job titles and dates on one line each, and use the standard section names the system expects.

Should I include a photo?

It depends on the market. ATS software ignores images, but in the US and UK photos are often discouraged, while they are common in parts of Europe and Asia. Resumake makes the photo optional.

How to check whether your PDF is readable.

  1. Open the exported PDF

    Use any PDF reader, including the one built into your browser.

  2. Try to select a line of text

    If you can highlight and copy a sentence, an ATS can usually extract it too.

  3. Paste it into a text editor

    If the paste looks like your resume in reading order, the text layer survived.
