An ATS-friendly resume builder that exports real text.

Resumake renders your resume with the browser's print engine, so the PDF contains a genuine text layer. Applicant tracking systems can read every line, and recruiters can copy it.

What makes a resume ATS-friendly?

What are the 3 things that make a PDF ATS-friendly?

It is 3 things: a real text layer instead of an image of text, conventional section headings such as Experience and Education, and a single-column layout without tables or decorative graphics that scramble reading order.

How does Resumake create the PDF?

It is generated locally by your browser's print engine: 1 file, no server-side converter, and no step where text could be flattened into an image. The output keeps a real text layer and clickable links.

Because the PDF is generated locally, there is no server-side converter that might flatten text into images.

The three templates are already structured for parsing, with a single column and standard section names. If keeping the file on your device matters too, read how the private mode works.

Is it guaranteed to pass every ATS?

It is not guaranteed: every ATS scores differently, and 1 image-based PDF fails all of them. What Resumake removes is the most common technical failure — a file with no extractable text.

What should I avoid in the content?

It is 3 things: icons in place of labels, contact details inside page headers or footers, and text baked into images. Keep job titles and dates on one line each, and use the standard section names the system expects.

Should I include a photo?

It depends on the market: in the US and UK 1 photo is often discouraged, while parts of Europe and Asia commonly expect one. ATS software ignores photos entirely, so a photo never affects parsing.

How to check whether your PDF is readable.

  1. Open the exported PDF

    Use any PDF reader, including the one built into your browser.

  2. Try to select a line of text

    If you can highlight and copy a sentence, an ATS can usually extract it too.

  3. Paste it into a text editor

    If the paste looks like your resume in reading order, the text layer survived.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a PDF ATS-friendly?

Three things: a real text layer instead of an image of text, conventional section headings such as Experience, Education and Skills, and a single-column layout without tables, icons or decorative graphics. All 3 Resumake templates use one column and those standard headings.

How does Resumake create the PDF?

Your browser own print engine renders the page, so the file is produced locally — there is no server-side converter that could flatten text into an image. The result is a PDF with a selectable text layer, clickable links, A4 or Letter paper, and margins from 10 mm to 20 mm.

Is it guaranteed to pass every ATS?

No builder can guarantee that, because every ATS scores differently and some reject anything unfamiliar. What Resumake removes is the most common technical failure: a PDF that is only an image and contains no extractable text.

How can I check my own PDF?

Open the exported PDF and try to select and copy one line. If the text highlights, an ATS can usually extract it too. If the whole page selects as a single image, it cannot.

Should I include a photo?

It depends on the market. ATS software ignores photos entirely, so a photo never affects parsing; in the US and UK it is often discouraged, while it is common in parts of Europe and Asia. Resumake makes the photo optional and crops it to a 3:4 ID ratio.

Can I change margins for a one-page fit?

Yes. There are 3 margin presets — 10 mm, 15 mm and 20 mm — plus a font scale from 80% to 120% in 5% steps. Page breaks are marked in the preview, so you can see exactly where the PDF will split.
