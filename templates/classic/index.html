Classic resume template: the formal, safe choice.

A centred header, navy rules under every section and a conventional order — the layout recruiters in established industries expect to see. Single column, real text, free.

What the Classic template looks like.

The name sits centred at the top in navy (24 pt, slightly letter-spaced), with the job title underneath in grey. Contact details follow on one centred line, separated by thin dots.

Every section heading is navy with a 1.5 px rule underneath. Dates line up on the right edge, so the eye can scan a career history in one pass.

The optional photo is centred under the name and framed with a light border — the placement European and Asian recruiters are used to.

You can still switch: the content stays exactly where it is whatever template you pick.

Three things that make this template look right.

  1. Tip 1

    Keep the header centred. The rule widths and the date alignment are calculated from the centred layout, so left-aligning it by hand breaks the rhythm.

  2. Tip 2

    Use one dark accent for the whole document. The default is #1e3a5f; #1f2937 or #7f1d1d also work. Gradients and two-colour schemes make the rules look sloppy.

  3. Tip 3

    Leave the section rules alone. They are what makes the structure obvious to a recruiter skimming on a phone — and they do not affect ATS parsing, because they are borders and not table cells.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Classic template ATS-friendly?

Yes. It is a single-column layout with standard section headings, and the PDF is exported as real text rather than an image, so applicant tracking systems can extract every line.

Can I change the colours in the Classic template?

Yes. The accent colour is a single setting: the name, the section headings and the rules all follow it. The default is #1e3a5f.

Does the Classic template support a photo?

Yes, it is optional. The photo is centred under the name, cropped to a 3:4 ID ratio, and can be aligned left, centre or right.

Does it work with a Chinese resume?

Yes. Chinese and English both work, and the English-only letter spacing is removed automatically so Chinese characters are not pushed apart.
