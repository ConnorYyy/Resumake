Minimal resume template: let the content carry the page.

No rules, no colour blocks, no boxes. A light-weight name, small uppercase section labels and generous whitespace — the layout that gets out of the way when your content is strong.

Use this template Compare all three

What the Minimal template looks like.

The name is left-aligned at 22 pt in a light weight with wide letter spacing, the job title in small uppercase grey underneath. Contact details sit on one line in grey.

Section headings are small (9.5 pt), bold and uppercase with wide tracking, and there is no rule or border under them — spacing alone separates the sections.

Dates are set in a lighter grey than the body text, so they recede and the role titles carry the hierarchy. The optional photo floats to the right.

Who the Minimal template works best for.

Good fit

  • Academia and research CVs
  • Senior individual contributors with long track records
  • Dense one-page resumes where every line counts
  • Roles judged on writing quality — editorial, strategy, policy
  • Resumes that will be read on screen rather than printed

Where it is a weaker fit

  • Public-sector or banking applications where a bare layout can read as unfinished
  • A first job with only one role — with very little content the whitespace looks like an empty page (use Classic or Modern instead)

You can still switch: the content stays exactly where it is whatever template you pick.

Three things that make this template look right.

  1. Tip 1

    Resist adding lines. The absence of rules is the design; if sections start to blur together, add space above the heading rather than a border.

  2. Tip 2

    Keep the name light. Bolding it at 22 pt turns the top of the page into a headline and breaks the hierarchy the rest of the template depends on.

  3. Tip 3

    Because nothing is boxed, keep every entry in the same order — company, role, dates — and keep dates short. Once the formats drift, a template without dividers stops being scannable.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Minimal template ATS-friendly?

Yes. It is the plainest of the three: no borders, no backgrounds, one column and standard section headings, and the PDF is exported as real text.

Does a Minimal resume look empty with little experience?

It can. The layout depends on content density, so with one role or no projects the whitespace dominates. For a first job, Classic or Modern fills the page better.

Can I add a photo to the Minimal template?

Yes. It floats to the right of the header and is cropped to a 3:4 ID ratio. Many academic and US applications look better without one.

Can I change the accent colour?

Yes, but only the headings use it — the body stays near-black. The default accent is #111827.
