Start without an account What's the catch?

What "no sign-up" actually means here.

Do I have to create an account?

No. There is no account, no email and no password. Click Open editor and start typing immediately.

Do I need an email address?

No. Resumake never asks for an email, sends no verification link and has no password to reset. Nothing arrives in your inbox.

Is there a trial, a credit card or a hidden step?

No. There is no trial, no card and no upsell step. Templates and PDF export are free and unlimited, and the download button is never locked.

Where is my resume saved if I do not have an account?

In your browser's local storage for resumake.online. Reopening the editor in the same browser restores your draft automatically.

What do I give up by not having an account?

Cross-device sync and password-based recovery. You move between devices with a backup JSON file instead of a login.

The honest trade-off.

No account means no cloud sync — and that cuts both ways.

You never wait for an email, never pick a password and never hand over a CV to a company. In exchange, your draft lives on one device until you export a backup file.

If you work across several computers every day, a cloud builder will feel more convenient. If you build a resume once and send it, the local model is faster and safer.

No account also means no "forgot password" moment.

There is nothing to recover, because there is no login. The equivalent safety net is the backup file: save it before you clear your browser or switch machines.
