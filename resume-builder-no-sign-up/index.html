A resume builder with no sign-up and no email. Just open it and type.

No account, no email address, no password and no verification link. The editor loads with a sample resume already in place, auto-saves to this browser as you type, and exports a free PDF in one of 3 templates.

Start without an account What's the catch?

What "no sign-up" actually means here.

Do I have to create an account?

No. There is no account, no email and no password. Click Open editor and the sample resume is already loaded, so you can start typing in one click.

Do I need an email address?

No. Resumake never asks for an email and there is no email field in the editor, so no verification link is sent and no password reset exists.

Is there a trial, a credit card or a hidden step?

No. There is no trial, no card and no upsell step. All 3 templates and unlimited PDF exports are free, and the download button is never locked.

Where is my resume saved if I do not have an account?

In your browser's own storage for resumake.online. Reopening the editor in the same browser restores the draft automatically; auto-save runs as you type.

No email address, no verification link.

Will I get marketing emails or a newsletter?

No. There is no mailing list to join, because no address is ever collected. The site cannot email you at all.

How do I move my resume to another device without logging in?

With the backup file. Save backup downloads a JSON file containing the resume and its settings, and Import restores it on any other browser or computer.

Can I use it if I do not have an email address at all?

Yes. Nothing in the editor, the export step or the backup and restore flow requires an email address.

The honest trade-off.

No account means no cloud sync — and that cuts both ways.

You never wait for an email, never pick a password and never hand over a CV to a company. In exchange, your draft lives on one device until you export a backup file.

If you work across several computers every day, a cloud builder will feel more convenient. If you build a resume once and send it, the local model is faster and safer.

For the privacy side of that trade-off, read what stays in your browser; for recovery without a login, see saving without an email address.

No account also means no "forgot password" moment.

There is nothing to recover, because there is no login. The equivalent safety net is the backup file: save it before you clear your browser or switch machines.
