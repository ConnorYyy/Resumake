Student resume template: no experience required.

A student resume is not a worse version of an experienced one — it is a different document. Education leads, projects do the work that jobs normally do, and everything is on one page. Here is how to build it.

What a student resume has to prove

There are 5 signals that replace work experience, and education is the first of them.

  • **Education first.** Degree, institution, expected graduation date, and relevant coursework. For a first job this is the strongest signal you have.
  • **Projects instead of jobs.** Two or three projects with a result each — a course project, a hackathon, a small tool, a research assistant role.
  • **Any work at all.** Part-time jobs, retail, tutoring and internships belong on the page; they prove reliability even when they are unrelated.
  • **GPA only when it helps.** Include it at 3.5/4.0 or above (or the local equivalent); below that, leave it out and let the coursework speak.
  • **One page, no exceptions.** A student resume never needs a second page.

How to write each section

Four sections on one page — that is the entire constraint, and everything else follows from it.

**Summary (optional, 1–2 lines).** Skip it unless you are targeting one specific role. “Third-year computer science student seeking a backend internship” is enough; anything longer is filler at this stage.

**Projects (2–3).** Same structure as a job: what you built, the stack, the constraint, the result. “Built a course-scheduling tool used by 200 students” is a result.

**Experience (1–3 bullets each).** Keep part-time jobs short. The point is not the job title; it is that you showed up and something measurable happened.

**Skills and activities.** Languages, tools, and one leadership item. Drop the high-school section entirely — it signals that nothing newer exists.

What do good student project bullets look like?

Built a timetable planner in Vue and SQLite; **200 students used it** during the spring semester.

Trained a classifier on 12k labelled images to **92% accuracy**, and documented the dataset for the next cohort.

Ran a 6-person study group for a 300-student course; **average exam score rose 11 points** versus the previous year.

Which template to use

Two templates suit a short student resume: Classic for structure, Modern for separating projects from education.

  • Classic template — the centred header and rules give a thin resume structure, which is exactly what a one-page student document needs.
  • Modern template — the accent bars separate projects from education clearly, which matters when both sections are the same length.

Minimal works too, but with only one or two entries per section it can look unfinished.

Frequently asked questions

Should a student resume include high school?

No, once you are in university. It signals that you have nothing more recent. The exception is a first-year application where the high school is genuinely the strongest item you have.

What GPA should I include?

Include it at 3.5/4.0 or the local equivalent (for example 85/100 or a First). Below that, omit it — most recruiters treat a missing GPA as neutral and a low one as a negative.

Do I need a summary as a student?

Usually not. If you include one, keep it to a single line naming the role you are applying for. Two-line summaries on student resumes are almost always filler.

Can I use this student resume template for an internship?

Yes. Internship applications are read the same way: education and projects first, one page, no decorative layout, PDF with real text so the application portal can parse it.

Should I include a photo?

In the US and UK, no. In parts of Europe and Asia it is still expected for some roles. The template supports an optional photo, so follow the convention of the country you are applying in.
