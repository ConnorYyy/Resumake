About Resumake

An independent, free resume builder that runs entirely in your browser. No account, no email, no upload — and no company behind it.

Why it exists

Every free resume builder I tried while job hunting had the same two walls: create an account, then pay to download the PDF you had just made. The document also left your machine — your name, phone number, employer history and sometimes a photo — into a database you could not see or delete.

Resumake is the version of that tool I wanted: open it, type, export. The PDF is produced by your browser, so there is no server-side copy to lose, leak or sell.

How it works

The landing pages are static HTML. The editor is a Vue 3 application that renders your resume into an iframe, and the PDF comes from your browser's own print engine — which is why the exported file contains real selectable text rather than an image.

Drafts are saved in IndexedDB inside your browser. There is no account and no server database; moving a resume between devices means downloading a JSON backup and importing it.

Who is behind it

One independent developer, working in the open. There is no company, no team, no investors and no advertising network — the code is published under the MIT license at github.com/ConnorYyy/Resumake, including the deployment workflow and the SEO checks.

Bugs and feature requests go through the GitHub issue tracker. There is no support queue, which is also why there is no account system.

Why it is free

The site is static files on Cloudflare Workers, so hosting costs are low and predictable. There is no resume database, no per-user storage and no sales team.

An optional Buy Me a Coffee link in the footer covers the rest. It unlocks nothing — every template, every export and every feature is available without paying, and there is no paid tier to upgrade to.

What it is not

It is not affiliated with resumake.io or any other similarly named service. The names are similar; the projects are not connected.

It is not a job board and not a CV-writing service. The optional AI button rewrites a single field you have already written; it does not invent experience on your behalf.

How your data is handled

Your resume stays in your browser. The site records anonymous counters (pages opened, PDFs exported) and crash reports, and the optional AI button sends only the field you are editing, capped at 600 characters.

The details, including the third parties involved, are listed in the privacy policy. If you want the technical version of where the draft lives, read how private mode works.

Frequently asked questions

Who owns the resume I create?

You do. Nothing is uploaded, so there is no server-side copy and no licence over your content. You can export the PDF or a JSON backup at any time.

Is Resumake affiliated with resumake.io?

No. Resumake.online is an independent project with a similar name. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by or connected to resumake.io.

Can I self-host it?

Yes. The repository is MIT licensed and includes the build and deploy configuration. A fork can run without the AI feature, because the button is hidden when no API key is configured.

How do I report a bug or request a feature?

Open an issue on the GitHub repository. There is no email support address, which is a consequence of not collecting email addresses anywhere on the site.
