One page resume template: what to cut, what to keep.

Almost every resume can fit on one page. The problem is that people cut the wrong things — they delete metrics and keep hobbies. This page is the order of operations, plus the three layout levers that buy space without removing content.

What should be cut first?

It is 5 cuts, in this order, and the first one usually frees 2 lines without losing anything a recruiter reads.

  • **Hobbies and interests.** Unless the hobby is directly relevant or genuinely unusual, it is the first thing to go.
  • **References and “available on request”.** Nobody asks, and it costs two lines.
  • **Oldest roles.** Keep the last 10–15 years. Anything earlier gets one line under “Earlier experience” or is dropped entirely.
  • **A summary that repeats the bullets.** If the summary says “experienced project manager” and the first role says the same thing with numbers, the summary is the weaker copy.
  • **Education detail.** After a few years, the degree, institution and year are enough; the coursework and GPA can go.

What should never be cut?

It is 3 things you cannot cut, even when the page runs 2 lines over.

  • **Numbers.** “Reduced churn 18%” is the reason you get the interview. Cutting it to save a line is a false economy.
  • **The most recent role.** This is the one a recruiter reads first. Everything else is allowed to be shorter.
  • **Anything the job description explicitly asks for.** If the posting names a certification and you have it, it stays even if the layout gets tight.

How do you fit more on one page?

It is 3 levers, and 2 of them change nothing about your content.

**Margins.** Going from 20 mm to 10 mm on A4 buys roughly six lines of text. The template exposes three presets — narrow (10 mm), normal (15 mm), wide (20 mm) — and the preview redraws immediately.

**Font scale.** 100% → 95% is usually invisible to a reader and recovers about four lines on a full page. The editor allows 80%–120% in 5% steps; below 90% it starts to look cramped, especially in print.

**The “fit to one page” control.** It nudges the font scale down until the content fits, instead of you guessing. If it has to go below 90%, the content is genuinely too long — go back to the cut list.

One warning: do not shrink margins and font at the same time and then send the PDF to a printer. 10 mm margins plus 90% text prints fine on most office printers but can clip on some. If the resume will be printed, keep margins at normal and cut one line instead.

When is a two page resume acceptable?

It is acceptable in 3 cases, and none of them is “my resume is long”.

Two pages are fine — expected, even — for 10+ years of experience, academic CVs with publications, and federal or medical roles that specify a longer format. In those cases the rule is not “one page”, it is “no page half empty”.

If the second page has fewer than five lines on it, you have a one-page resume with a formatting problem, not a two-page resume.

Which template fits on one page most easily

It is 3 templates ranked by density: Minimal fits most easily, Classic next, and Modern takes about 10% more vertical space.

  • Minimal template — the tightest of the three: small uppercase headings, no rules, minimal spacing. Most likely to fit as-is.
  • Classic template — rules and a centred header cost a little height, but the structure keeps a dense page readable.
  • Modern template — the colour block and accent bars take about 10% more vertical space. Budget for it, or set the font scale to 95%.

All three show page-break markers in the preview, so you can see the split before exporting.

Frequently asked questions

Is a one page resume always better?

No. It is better for candidates with under about 10 years of experience, where a second page usually means the first one was padded. For senior, academic and federal applications, two pages are normal.

How many bullet points fit on one page?

Roughly 12–16 bullets at 10.5 pt with 15 mm margins, across three or four roles. If you are far above that, the content needs cutting, not shrinking.

Does shrinking the font hurt ATS parsing?

No. Applicant tracking systems read the text layer, not the visual size. A 90% font scale is still machine-readable — but keep it readable for humans, who are the second filter.

Can I delete older jobs to fit one page?

Yes, once they are more than about 10–15 years old. Keep the most recent roles in full, and either compress the older ones to a single line each or move them to an “Earlier experience” line.
